# Writing rules for bullets

## The formula
**Verb + what you did + how or at what scale + result.** One or two lines (under ~200 characters). Put the strongest proof early.

- Weak: "Responsible for improving the website."
- Strong: "Rebuilt the checkout page in React, cutting load time from 4.1s to 1.3s and lifting completed orders by 9%."

## Numbers
Ask for them in one batch. Useful: users, revenue, speed, cost saved, team size, scale (requests, files, clients), time saved, ranking, adoption. If the user has no number, write a specific concrete detail instead (what shipped, who used it, what changed). **Never invent or round up.** Ranges and "about" are fine when the user says them.

## Verbs
Use plain, strong, varied openers: built, shipped, designed, cut, grew, led, wrote, automated, launched, migrated, reduced, negotiated, taught, ran, fixed, started, won. Do not start more than two bullets with the same verb.
Honest verbs only: "led" needs people or ownership; "founded" needs a company or product the user truly started; "helped" is fine when it is true, but say what.

## Voice
Sound like the person. Reuse their own phrases and level of formality. Short sentences beat long ones. Prefer concrete nouns (a checkout page) to abstract ones (digital solutions).

## Avoid (the checker flags these)
- Buzzwords: passionate, results-driven, dynamic, team player, detail-oriented, self-starter, synergy, rockstar, ninja, guru.
- AI-sounding words: delve, tapestry, seamlessly, cutting-edge, robust, testament, elevate, unlock, harness, spearhead, leverage, holistic, transformative, empower, "in today's".
- Em dashes. More than two in a resume reads as machine-written. Use commas or full stops.
- First person (I, my). Resumes imply it.
- Weak openers: responsible for, worked on, helped, assisted, participated in, involved in, duties included.

## Summary
Two or three sentences: who you are, your strongest proof, what you want next. No adjectives without evidence. Skip the summary for a very short resume if it repeats the bullets.

## Skills
Group with plain labels (Languages, Frameworks, Tools, Growth). Only list what the user can discuss in an interview. No skill bars, no ratings.

## Length
One page for under ten years of experience. Older or less relevant roles shrink to one line before relevant ones are cut.
